# Drive With Dave — future possibilities (document only, nothing here is built)

## More Daves (each needs a reason to exist, not just stat changes)
* **Nervous learner** — have: meltdowns, early stops. Add: indicating for no reason, asking permission.
* **Cocky mate** — have: refuses, assumes, blames. Add: shortcuts that aren't, overtaking.
* **Elderly driver** — slow reactions, excellent judgement, selective hearing ("Eh?"), drives in the middle of the road.
* **Teenager** — fast hands, no judgement, music too loud (voice commands get worse).
* **Terrible taxi driver** — knows "a better way", takes it, charges you.
* **Overconfident racing driver** — late braking, heel-and-toe sound design, can't park at all.
Risk: dozens of Daves dilute character. Four to six strong ones beat twenty weak ones.

## Situations (ranked by how many decisions per minute they create)
1. **Car park / multi-storey** — constant left/right/stop/reverse, pedestrians, tight spaces. Probably the best next level.
2. **City centre** — junctions every 50 m, buses, cyclists, lanes.
3. **Driving test** — an examiner in the back scoring *Dave*; you're coaching.
4. **Airport run** — time pressure, signs only you can read.
5. **Roundabouts** — "which exit" is a natural instruction problem.
6. **Escaping danger** — comic chase; urgency all the time (risk: becomes a stress simulator).
7. **Night / snow** — lower attention, lower grip; reuses everything.
8. **Motorway** — merging is good; long stretches are not.
9. **Road trip** — chain of short scenarios rather than one long drive.
10. **Towing a caravan** — reversing a trailer under instruction is genuinely hard and funny.

## Other vehicles
* **Van / bus** — same sim, bigger, worse visibility; bus adds passengers who complain.
* **Forklift** — rear steering makes Dave's mistakes alien and funny.
* **Truck with trailer** — the caravan problem, larger.
* **Boat / plane** — different physics; only if the core works first.

## Multiplayer
* **Driver + navigator**: player A drives with the full awkward Normal Driving controls; player B
  sees the map and must instruct by voice. Asymmetric information is the whole game.
* **Navigator vs. navigator**: two passengers with conflicting instructions; Dave decides who to listen to.
* **Couch party**: one Dave, many backseat drivers with buttons; urgency comes from the crowd.
* **Streamer mode**: chat votes are commands (urgency = vote volume).
Dave's systems double as the "AI driver" when a human slot is empty.

## Systems worth adding before any of the above
* Coach-parking mode (fine LEFT/RIGHT/STOP/BACK during manoeuvres).
* Cross traffic at junctions so GO can be wrong.
* Larger authored dialogue set with callbacks ("Like last time?").
* Dave remembering *you* across runs (lightly — not a relationship RPG).
